# Brand Voice & Tone Reference Guide

## Voice Archetypes

Match the brand's personality to one or more of these foundational archetypes. Each archetype comes with built-in language guidance.

---

### 1. The Expert / Authority
**Who:** Knows their field deeply; speaks with confidence and precision.  
**Industries:** Finance, legal, healthcare, B2B software, consulting.  
**Sounds like:** Clear, factual, substantive. No fluff.  
**Words to use:** "proven," "data-backed," "essential," "precisely," "optimized"  
**Words to avoid:** "amazing," "awesome," "totally," "super easy," "game-changer"  
**Example:** "Our compliance engine processes 10,000 regulatory checks per second — accuracy you can rely on."

---

### 2. The Friend / Companion
**Who:** Warm, conversational, on your side. Never condescending.  
**Industries:** Consumer apps, food, lifestyle, wellness, pet brands.  
**Sounds like:** Casual but genuine. Empathetic and warm.  
**Words to use:** "we," "you," "together," "let's," "here for you," "whenever you need"  
**Words to avoid:** "leverage," "synergize," "utilize," "per the above," "as per"  
**Example:** "We know mornings can be rough. That's why we made it ridiculously easy to get started."

---

### 3. The Visionary / Inspirer
**Who:** Forward-thinking, big-picture, motivating. Believes in a better future.  
**Industries:** Tech startups, sustainability, education, nonprofits.  
**Sounds like:** Aspirational but grounded. Calls people toward something bigger.  
**Words to use:** "imagine," "build," "transform," "future," "possible," "together we"  
**Words to avoid:** "simply," "just," "basic," "ordinary," any defeatist language  
**Example:** "The tools you build today will define how the next generation learns. Let's build them right."

---

### 4. The Rebel / Challenger
**Who:** Challenges the status quo. Provocative but purposeful.  
**Industries:** Streetwear, fitness disruptors, challenger brands, creative agencies.  
**Sounds like:** Direct, punchy, unafraid of controversy. Short sentences.  
**Words to use:** "bold," "refuse," "break," "real," "against," "no excuses," "enough"  
**Words to avoid:** "traditional," "established," "standard," "conventional," wishy-washy hedging  
**Example:** "Everyone else optimized for vanity metrics. We optimized for your results."

---

### 5. The Guide / Teacher
**Who:** Patient, clear, educational. Empowers through knowledge.  
**Industries:** EdTech, healthcare, SaaS onboarding, financial literacy, parenting.  
**Sounds like:** Step-by-step, approachable. Uses analogies. Never talks down.  
**Words to use:** "here's how," "simply," "step by step," "think of it as," "you've got this"  
**Words to avoid:** "obviously," "as you know," "clearly," "trivially" (condescending)  
**Example:** "Budgeting can feel overwhelming — so let's break it down into five simple steps."

---

### 6. The Creator / Artist
**Who:** Expressive, aesthetic-forward, passionate about craft.  
**Industries:** Design studios, fashion, art platforms, music, luxury goods.  
**Sounds like:** Evocative, sensory-rich, unconventional. Can break grammar rules intentionally.  
**Words to use:** "crafted," "curated," "texture," "light," "presence," "made with intention"  
**Words to avoid:** "efficient," "scalable," "leverage," "ROI," overly functional language  
**Example:** "Each piece begins not with a sketch, but with a feeling."

---

## Tone Adjustment Chart

The same brand voice shifts tone by context. Here's how to adjust:

| Scenario | Direction | Notes |
|----------|-----------|-------|
| Marketing / advertising | Turn up energy, inspiration | Lead with benefit, not feature |
| Website homepage | Confident, welcoming | Balance aspiration with clarity |
| Product descriptions | Clear, specific, enticing | Sensory detail + practical benefit |
| Onboarding / tutorials | Patient, encouraging | Short sentences, celebrate progress |
| Error messages | Calm, helpful, never blame | "Something went wrong" not "You did it wrong" |
| Customer support | Empathetic, decisive | Acknowledge → explain → resolve |
| Social media | Conversational, timely | Platform-appropriate, can be playful |
| Press / investor material | Professional, credible | Data-driven, precise |
| Celebration / milestones | Warm, genuine, joyful | Don't oversell, let the moment speak |
| Crisis communication | Honest, measured, accountable | Lead with acknowledgment, then action |

## Common Voice Mistakes to Avoid

### Corporate Speak (Kills authenticity)
❌ "We leverage synergistic solutions to optimize stakeholder outcomes."  
✅ "We help teams work better together."

### Hollow Superlatives (Loses trust)
❌ "The world's most powerful, revolutionary, game-changing platform."  
✅ "The platform that cut our customers' processing time by 40%."

### Passive Voice (Dilutes impact)
❌ "Mistakes were made."  
✅ "We made a mistake. Here's what we're doing about it."

### Being Overly Casual (Loses authority)
❌ "Yo, our app is literally so fire 🔥🔥🔥"  
✅ "Our app is fast, smart, and ready when you are."

### Fearmongering (Alienates)
❌ "Without our product, your business will fail."  
✅ "Thousands of businesses use us to stay ahead of the curve."

### Wishy-Washy Hedging (Weakens)
❌ "We kind of hope this might possibly help you maybe feel a bit better."  
✅ "This will help you feel better — or your money back."

## Writing Style Reference

### Active vs. Passive Voice
| Passive (avoid) | Active (prefer) |
|-----------------|-----------------|
| "The report was generated by the system" | "The system generated the report" |
| "Mistakes were made" | "We made a mistake" |
| "Your order has been shipped by us" | "We shipped your order" |

### Sentence Length Guidance
- **Hero/headline copy:** 3–7 words
- **Subheadings:** 5–12 words
- **Body copy:** Mix short (8–12 word) and medium (15–20 word) sentences
- **Long-form content:** Vary rhythm — short punchy sentence. Then a slightly longer one that adds context or nuance. Never more than 30 words in a single sentence.

### Punctuation Personality
| Style | Best for |
|-------|---------|
| Em dash — for emphasis | Adds drama; use sparingly |
| Ellipsis... | Implies continuation; avoid in professional copy |
| Exclamation marks! | Celebrations and CTAs only; 1 per paragraph max |
| Oxford comma | Always use it to avoid ambiguity |
| Bold for emphasis | Prefer over italics for web content |
| ALL CAPS | Headlines and very short tags only; never body text |

## Inclusivity & Language Guidelines

### Gendered Language
- Use "they/them" as a singular pronoun when gender is unknown
- Avoid "guys" as a group address — use "folks," "everyone," "team," "you all"
- Avoid gendered job titles — use "firefighter" not "fireman," "flight attendant" not "stewardess"

### Ableist Language to Avoid
| Avoid | Use Instead |
|-------|------------|
| "crazy idea" | "bold idea," "unconventional idea" |
| "blind to the issue" | "overlooked the issue" |
| "falling on deaf ears" | "being ignored" |
| "lame" | "weak," "poor," "ineffective" |

### Cultural Sensitivity
- Avoid idioms that don't translate internationally ("hit it out of the park" → "achieve great results")
- Avoid culturally specific references unless targeting a specific culture intentionally
- When in doubt, choose the clearer, more universal phrasing
